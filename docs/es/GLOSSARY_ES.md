# Spanish glossary

The terms Deal Desk uses in Spanish, with the reason for each. Where the CFPB or FTC publishes the Spanish word, we use theirs, because that is what a person will meet on a government page, a lender's disclosure or a counselling session. Terms marked **open** are for the native reviewer to settle (see `docs/ES_REVIEW_PROCESS.md`).

Register: formal "usted" always. Decimal point for decimals (7.9%). Dollar sign before the figure ($1,200). No dashes for pauses.

Sources checked: the CFPB's Spanish auto-loan pages (consumerfinance.gov/es), the FTC's Spanish "Cómo comprar un auto" consumer pages (consumidor.ftc.gov), and the NHTSA's Spanish recall pages (nhtsa.gov/es). Re-check these pages when the glossary is next reviewed; wording on government sites changes.

## Words we avoid

- "Tú" forms of any kind.
- "Vendedor" for the dealership as a company; use "concesionario". "Vendedor" is the person.
- Idioms that do not travel ("hacer la vista gorda", "pagar los platos rotos").
- "Cuota" alone for a fee, since in much of Latin America it means a monthly instalment.
