How to find your car's real MSRP read first

Dealer websites almost never show a plain, unmistakable "MSRP" label — they show whatever price sells the car. Here's how to tell what you're actually looking at.

What the listing saysWhat it actually means
"MSRP", "Retail Price", "Sticker Price"This is the real MSRP — use it as-is
"Internet Price", "Our Price", "Sale Price", "Advertised Price"This is the dealer's asking price, already discounted or marked up — not MSRP
"You Pay", "Final Price", "After Rebates"Often assumes rebates you may not qualify for (military, loyalty, etc.) — don't take this at face value

Three reliable ways to get the true MSRP:

  1. Find the window sticker / Monroney label. Most dealer listings have a "Window Sticker" link or button on the vehicle's page — it's the manufacturer's own itemized price sheet for that exact VIN, and it's the most trustworthy source available.
  2. Use the manufacturer's own "Build & Price" tool. Search "[the car's brand] build and price" — every manufacturer has one on their official site. Enter the exact trim and options and it shows true MSRP directly from the source, independent of any dealer.
  3. Cross-check on Edmunds.com or KBB.com. Search the year, make, model, and trim — both sites list manufacturer MSRP by trim, separate from what any specific dealer is asking.
How this worksheet is organized quick tour

Once you hit Start, you'll move through three steps:

  1. Deal. The top half of the screen holds your inputs — swipe sideways through car price, loan terms, fees, add-ons, and trade-in. If a card runs long, scroll inside just that card. The bottom half is your running total — always on screen at the same time, no scrolling needed to see it.
  2. Verify. Cross-check anything the dealer quotes you — a marked-up interest rate, a discount-for-higher-rate offer, or a monthly payment that hides a higher price.
  3. Script. A copy-ready summary of your numbers to read straight to the salesperson.

Every number field has a slider for fast adjustment, plus a box if you want to type an exact figure. Small "?" icons explain any term you're unsure of.
